# Reddle: Letterboxd for books

A social website for logging and reviewing books, like Letterboxd is for films.

## Goal for version 1

A user can sign up with a username, upload a profile picture, search for a book,
log it with a star rating and review, follow other users, and see a feed of what
the people they follow are reading.

## Decisions made

- **Friend system:** FOLLOW, like Letterboxd. No approval needed. The feed is the logs from people you follow.
- **Book library:** do not preload books. Search Open Library live (chosen over Google Books because it needs no key). A book's external ID is its Open Library work id, like `OL45804W`, and book pages are `book/OL45804W`. Comics and manga also come from Google Books (ids `GB` + volume id, see Universes). The first time anyone logs or reviews a book, save a copy (title, author, cover, description, ISBN, external ID) into our own database. Reviews and scores attach to that saved copy.
- **Ratings:** half stars from 0.5 to 5. One log per user per book to start with. A log may have no rating (empty).
- **Username:** chosen at sign up, unique, lowercase letters, numbers and underscores only, 3 to 20 characters. It becomes the profile link (yoursite.com/username).
- **Profile picture:** JPG, PNG or WEBP up to 5 MB. Cropped to a square from the centre and resized to 256 x 256 JPEG in the browser (canvas), then uploaded to the public Supabase Storage bucket `avatars` as `USERID/avatar.jpg` (one picture per user, replaced on each upload). The public link plus `?v=timestamp` is saved in `profiles.avatar_url`, so browsers show the new picture instead of an old cached copy. No picture: grey circle with the first letter of the username (pure CSS).
- **Sign up:** email and password, plus Google sign in.
- **Staying logged in:** logins are remembered on each device (`persistSession`, `autoRefreshToken` in `js/supabase-client.js`; the login lives in the browser's local storage) until the person presses Log out. Log out only logs out that device (`signOut({ scope: "local" })`). Safari may forget it after about 7 days without a visit (its own privacy rule for sites like ours).
- **Usernames at sign up:** the chosen username is stored on the login account (`user_metadata.username`). A database trigger (`supabase/step2-profile-trigger.sql`) creates the profile row the moment the account is created; it skips bad or taken usernames instead of failing the sign up. As a backup, `ensureProfile` in `js/auth.js` creates it on first login. Any logged-in user without a profile (e.g. first Google sign in, or their username got taken meanwhile) is sent to `choose-username.html` by `js/auth.js`.
- **Clean addresses:** profiles are `u/USERNAME`, books are `book/OL45804W`, authors are `author/OL23919A` (`authorLink()`, fallback `author.html?a=`) (from the site's main folder: `https://cflo13.github.io/Reddle/u/maria`). `profileLink()`, `bookLink()` and `authorLink()` (all in auth.js) make them; opened as local files they fall back to `profile.html?u=` / `book.html?id=`. How it works: GitHub Pages has no such files, so it serves `404.html`; a small script at the top of 404.html works out the site's main folder (`/Reddle/` on github.io, `/` on a custom domain) and writes `<base href>` for it; if the address is `u/...` or `book/...`, `js/router.js` fetches `profile.html` / `book.html`, swaps its head and body in, and loads its scripts in order, so the address stays clean. Otherwise it's a real Page not found. Every other page has `<base href="./">`, so relative links keep working after the address changes. Pages read their value with `pageParam("u")` / `pageParam("id")` (query or path). Old links (`profile.html?u=`, `book.html?id=`) still work and switch the address bar to the clean one with `useCleanAddress()` (no reload). Limit: clean addresses are served with a 404 status by GitHub Pages, so some link-preview apps may show no preview for them (previews were generic for books and profiles anyway). Genre pages stay `genre.html?g=`.
- **Profile links (old usernames):** If no current user has that name, the profile page looks it up in `old_usernames` and sends the visitor on to the person's new name.
- **Back to where you were:** clicking any Log in / Sign up link remembers the current page in local storage for 2 hours (`rememberReturnPage`, a click listener in `js/auth.js`). After logging in, `goBackAfterLogin()` goes there (default: home page). New users still see the welcome page first; its last button becomes "Back to where I was". Google sign in and email-confirmation links land on the home page, which forwards to the remembered page. `safeReturnPath()` only allows our own site's pages (never other websites, never login/sign up pages).
- **Resend confirmation email:** `makeResendButton()` (auth.js) appears after sign up when the email needs confirming, and on the login page after "Email not confirmed". Once a minute at most (countdown on the button); Supabase's own rate limit gets a plain message.
- **Installable app (PWA):** `manifest.webmanifest` (name/short name Reddle, standalone, background #1d1714, theme #271f1a, start and scope `./` so it works at reddle.org and under /Reddle/; icons `images/icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, maskable `icon-maskable-192/512.png`, made from favicon.svg). `sw.js` (registered from `js/auth.js` on https only) keeps our own pages, CSS, JS and pictures: pages internet-first, files saved-copy-first; older `?v=` copies are replaced. It never keeps other websites (Supabase, Open Library, Google, the CDN) or the login/account pages. No internet and no saved copy: `offline.html` (self-contained, no links to other files). Install app (`startInstall()` in auth.js): the browser's own install box where there is one (Chrome, Edge, Android), otherwise a how-to sheet (`openInstallHelp()`, steps for iPhone/iPad, Android or computers). Offered in the footer (Chrome/Edge/Android and Apple devices), in the phone menu, and on phones as a small home page card "Reddle on your home screen" (Install / Not now; Not now hides it for 60 days, local storage `reddle-install-card-hidden`). None of these show inside the installed app or on non-https addresses (localhost allowed for testing). Inside the installed app (`html.installed-app`): no bounce/pull-to-refresh at the page ends (`overscroll-behavior-y: none`), no text selection on long-pressed bar/header buttons, and a Back arrow left of the logo on every page but home (`addAppBackButton()`; iPhones have no Back there). Status bar: `theme-color` = the header colour (`--surface`, switched with day/night); iPhones use `black-translucent` (white clock over the page), so a fixed strip in `--status-bar` (night #271f1a, day dark leather #3a2a20) sits behind the clock (`body::before`, height = the safe area, 0 elsewhere). iPhone tags (`apple-mobile-web-app-*`, `apple-touch-icon` now a full square) are in every page's head. To throw away every saved copy, change `CACHE` in `sw.js`.
- **Live search suggestions:** `js/search-suggest.js` (on every page, after auth.js) shows a box under the header search bar while typing: Books (5, Open Library), Authors (3, Open Library author search, small photo or initial), Readers (4, our profiles), then "See all results for ...". Starts at 2 characters, waits 250 ms, cancels/ignores older searches, remembers answers per word for the page. Arrow keys, Enter (opens the highlighted one, or the search page), Escape, click outside closes. On phones the box spans the screen (8px margins), rows are 48px tall. The same file has the shared author helpers (`searchAuthors`, `authorPhotoUrl`, `makeAuthorAvatar`, `yearsText`), so its constant is `SUGGEST_OPEN_LIBRARY` (top-level `const` names must not repeat across our scripts).
- **Author pages:** `author.html` + `js/author.js`, all from Open Library, nothing saved: photo (or initial), name, birth/death years, bio (plain text or `{value}`; links, HTML and source notes stripped; 4 lines with Read more; hidden if empty), Wikipedia link (only real wikipedia.org addresses), "Books by this author" (Open Library search by author, most editions first, 24 at a time, duplicate titles removed). Book pages turn "by NAME" into an author link after asking Open Library for the work's author (`linkAuthorName()` in book.js). The full search page has an Authors section above the books.
- **Design (the look): a quiet reading nook.** Warm, calm, bookish, unhurried: paper, leather, lamplight, wood shelves. Lots of breathing room, soft edges, quiet colours, nothing shouting. Clean = few borders, few competing colours, clear spacing. Rules for all future work:

The only literal colours outside the tokens: the tiny theme script in each page's head and `manifest.webmanifest` (they run before the CSS), `offline.html` (self-contained) and the email HTML in the Edge Function.

Both are fonts people already have, so nothing downloads and it works offline.

  - **Shapes:** `--radius-small` 6, `--radius` 10, `--radius-large` 16 (cards), `--radius-pill` (buttons, chips, search box). Cards: surface colour + soft warm shadow (`--card-shadow`), no border. Lines (`--border`) only where they separate things.
  - **Buttons:** filled accent pills for the main action; quiet ones (`.button-secondary`) are transparent with a soft outline and a panel-coloured hover; link-buttons are muted text that turns accent on hover. One focus ring everywhere (accent).
  - **Reading text:** reviews, book descriptions, author bios and diary snippets use the serif at 1.05rem with 1.65 line height (like a page of a book). Interface text stays sans.
  - **Covers** have a book-like shape (spine side tighter) and a soft two-layer shadow; missing covers are panel-coloured with the title in serif. **Chips** (genre tags, pills, choices, people) are panel-coloured pills with no border. **Lists** (log cards, search results, diary) are separated by one soft `--border` line, never boxed.
  - **Logo/icons:** three spines in olive #98a56b, amber #d9a35f and brick #c4705a on a lamplight tile #1d1714 with a cream shelf (`favicon.svg`; the PNG icons and `images/share.png` are made from it with a browser script in Claude's scratch folder).
  - **Day/night switch:** the head script sets `data-theme` before the CSS loads (no flash); pressing the sun/moon fades colours for 0.4s (`.theme-fading`, added by `showTheme(theme, true)`) and the icons cross-fade with a small turn. Reduced-motion users get no animation.
- **Universes (Books, Comics, Manga):** three modes of one site. Books is the cosy default; Comics and Manga look different (and, from part b, only show their own world). The choice is in local storage `reddle-universe`; the head script sets `data-universe="books|comics|manga"` on `<html>` before the CSS loads (no flash) and picks the status bar colour per universe and theme. Switch (`setUpUniverseSwitch()`, `goToUniverse()`, `showUniverse()`, `currentUniverse()`, `UNIVERSES` in auth.js): a tilted pill in the header (`#universe-button`, in the page template; icon only on phones). In Books it opens a small menu "Enter Comic Mode" / "Switch to Manga Mode"; in Comics and Manga it says "Back to Books". The footer has the same links (`.footer-universe`) and the phone menu too. Any element with `data-go-universe="comics"` switches. Switching saves the choice and reloads (a 0.16s squash first, none with reduced motion), so every row and search on the page comes from the new universe; `showUniverse()` re-skins in place without a reload. Labels are shown by CSS from `data-universe` (`.u-away` / `.u-home`), so the right one is there at once. Works logged out.
  - **Look:** each universe overrides the colour tokens in style.css, night and day (`:root[data-universe="comics"]`, `...[data-theme="light"]`). Comics: ink blue + yellow at night, newsprint cream + comic blue + red dots by day; `--ink` outlines, `--card-shadow` = bold outline + offset shadow, `--button-shadow`, halftone dots (`--page-pattern`, radial gradients), cards as speech bubbles (an ink tail at the bottom left on `.card`, `.pick-card`, `.prompt-card`, `.install-card`, `.spotlight-card`), headings in capitals in "Reddle Comic" (an `@font-face` that only uses fonts already on the device via `local()`: Impact, Helvetica Neue Condensed Black, Avenir Next Condensed Heavy, Futura Condensed, Arial Black; declared for every weight so it's never fake-bolded), a "COMICS" sticker next to the logo. Manga: black and white with one red, fine screentone dots, square corners (radius tokens 0, pills 2px), 2px panel outlines, headings in a heavy Japanese/Western sans with a red bar, a "Manga" label next to the logo. Font tokens: `--font-display` (headings, logo, big numbers; changes per universe), `--display-weight`, `--display-transform`, `--display-spacing`, `--heading-shadow`; `--font-reading` (reviews, descriptions, bios, posts) stays the calm serif at 1.05rem / 1.65 in every universe. Contrast checked for text, muted, accent, error and success on bg, surface and panel in all six palettes (4.5:1 or better).
  - **Strict comics/manga rules (October 2026 fix; `universeGenresFromSubjects()` and `googleUniverseGenres()` in sources.js).** Why: Open Library gives a work the subjects of ALL its editions, so classics with one comic adaptation (Hamlet, The Hobbit, 1984, Wuthering Heights...) carry "Graphic novels" among 100+ subjects and used to count as comics (and vanished from Books). The exact rules:
    - Comic format subjects: comic books/strips, comic strips, comics & graphic novels, graphic novels/books, cartoons and comics, comic and graphic books, bandes dessinées, webcomics, webtoons, manga, manhwa, manhua, Japanese/Korean/Chinese comics, "superhero comic...", "(Comic strip)", `form:graphic novel|manga|comic`, a subject that is just "comic(s)", and NYT graphic lists (`nyt:...graphic...`). Not "comic novels"/"comic fiction" (funny prose).
    - Comic (Open Library): a `form:` tag, OR 2+ comic subjects making up at least 12% of all its subjects, OR 1 when it has 4 subjects or fewer. Otherwise it's a book.
    - Manga: a comic with manga-specific subjects (manga, manhwa, manhua, webtoon, shonen/shojo/seinen/josei, "east asian style", Japanese comics; never `nyt:` tags): `form:manga`, or 2+, or 1 that is 30%+ of its comic subjects, or 1 plus a creator name in Japanese/Korean/Chinese script (author names and Open Library's alternative names). Light novels need 2. Otherwise a comic is Comics (Tintin, Dog Man stay Comics; a few thinly tagged manga like Whistle! land in Comics, never in Books). Both only with 2+ Western signs (superheroes, American/European comics, bandes dessinées).
    - Google Books: Comics only when a category is itself a comics category (starts with "Comics & Graphic Novels", or "Young Adult/Juvenile Fiction / Comics & Graphic Novels"); Manga when that category says manga/manhwa/manhua/webtoon. Never from what we searched for, and books ABOUT comics ("Literary Criticism / Comics...", "Art / Cartooning") are books.
    - Books mode excludes both. Checked against real Open Library answers fetched through the live database (`pg_net`): all 60 top "comics" results that were classics now count as books; Watchmen, Saga, Persepolis, Berserk, Naruto, One Piece, Nana etc. stay right.
  - **Curated seed lists (`js/universe-seeds.js`, loaded with books.js; `seeds-resolved.json`):** 80 comics and 82 manga, each title + creator(s) + a few of our genres (text only, no logos or artwork). The site never searches for them (that made Comics/Manga pages take 9-13 s): **`tools/resolve-seeds.mjs`** runs on GitHub every Monday 05:23 UTC and on demand (`.github/workflows/seeds.yml`, "Resolve seed lists", Run workflow button). It loads the site's own `sources.js` + `universe-seeds.js` in Node (so the rules are identical), asks Open Library for each title (`title=&author=`, then `title=` alone for creators filed under another script; sorted by editions; about one request a second with a Reddle User-Agent), both searches every time; of the results whose title matches (whole words; a volume or subtitle may follow; "Batman: Year One" also matches "Year One"), whose creator matches (any name or a 4+ letter part, incl. Open Library's alternative names) and which pass the strict rules for that universe it keeps the best (`score()`: exact title 5, volume 1 4, a subtitle or edition 3, later volumes about 1, half a point for a cover; spin-offs such as anime guides, ani-manga, cookbooks, film books or abridged editions (`SPIN_OFF`) are never picked), and saves `{ external_id, title, author, cover_url, isbns, genres (book's + seed's), seed }` per universe in **`seeds-resolved.json`** (committed; written only when the books changed). Unmatched titles are skipped and listed in the log. If more than a quarter of the requests fail (Open Library unreachable), the old file is kept. Google Books isn't used there (its terms don't allow stored copies); never descriptions. The site fetches the file once per page (`loadResolvedSeeds()`, `seeds-resolved.json?v=SITE VERSION`; the service worker keeps a copy, refreshed in the background) and `makeSeedStream(universe, genre)` hands out books in today's order (date-seeded shuffle, `shuffleForToday()`), instantly. Used for: popular.html (Reddle this week, then, once the universe has 24+ logged books (`SEED_UNTIL`, `universeLoggedCount()` in books.js), Reddle's most logged ever, then the seeds), the home Popular this week row (same), Comics/Manga genre rows (Reddle top-rated + seeds of that genre; no Open Library search on the home page), genre pages (seed tiles show at once, then the strict Open Library search adds to them; Load more continues the search), Books you might like (Reddle + seeds), and an empty Comics/Manga feed ("Start with these ..."). After adding seed titles, run the workflow so they appear.
  - **Speed (Comics/Manga):** slow-connection test with stand-ins (400 ms round trip, 1.6 Mbps, 1 s per Open Library search, 300 ms per database call): first covers on popular.html, home and genre pages went from 8.5-12.7 s to 2.7-3.9 s on a first visit (Open Library calls on the home page 53-57 -> 0-1); what's left is downloading our own CSS/JS once, which the service worker keeps for later visits. `seeds-resolved.json` is preloaded by the head script in Comics/Manga (`<link rel="preload" as="fetch">`), and popular.js asks the database and the seed file at the same time. The only Open Library searches left on these pages: New and noteworthy (12 results, only when scrolled to it) and the genre page's extra results.
  - **Google Books key (optional):** `GOOGLE_BOOKS_KEY` in config.js (empty now). Without a key Google answered "quota exceeded" for both this sandbox and Supabase's server, so `googleJson()` stops calling Google for the rest of the visit after a 429/403. Owner steps: `GOOGLE-BOOKS-KEY.md` (a browser key locked to reddle.org and the Books API is safe to be public).
  - **Caches:** home rows are remembered per universe and per `ROWS_VERSION` (home.js; bump it when the way rows are filled changes). The service worker never stores Open Library, Google or Supabase answers.
  - **Which universe a book is in (part b):** decided by its genres (`bookUniverses()`, `bookInUniverse()` in `js/sources.js`): `manga` in its genres = Manga, `comics` = Comics, neither = Books; a book with both is in both. `comics` ("Comics & graphic novels") and `manga` are genres in `GENRES` with `universe:` set; they never appear in genre lists (`listedGenres()`: genre pages, buttons, the taste questionnaire, taste rows). The old `graphic_novels` genre became `comics` (`GENRE_ALIASES`, and `step24` changed saved books and favourite genres; the database still accepts the old slug). `genresFromSubjects()` spots manga from manga, manhwa, manhua, webtoon(s), shonen/shojo/seinen/josei, "Japanese comics" or comics + Japan/Korea; comics from comics, comic books/strips, graphic novels, superheroes, webcomics, bandes dessinées. Manga isn't also comics unless there's a clear Western-comics sign (superheroes, American/European comics). "Graphic novels" and "nonfiction" don't count as signs of fiction (so Maus is comics + biography). Comics and manga always stay, plus up to 4 other genres.
  - **Filtering:** database queries go through `onlyThisUniverse(query, column, universe)` (Books: `not genres ov {comics,manga}`; Comics/Manga: `genres contains`), on `books_with_stats`, `books`, `trending_books_week` (now has genres) and on logs with `books!inner(..., genres)` + `"books.genres"`. Used by the home rows (For you, taste rows, People are talking about, Popular this week, New and noteworthy, genre rows, popular genres), popular.html, genre.html, genres.html (counts), Books you might like. `fillRow` also drops any candidate whose genres are from another universe. Home rows remember results per universe. Comics/Manga rows add " comics"/" manga" to genre names ("Popular in Fantasy manga"). genre.html?g=comics / ?g=manga switch to that universe and show all of it. The search page, header suggestions (heading = the universe's name) and the post book picker use `searchUniverseBooks()`; search boxes say "Search comics or people" (`showSearchPlaceholders()`). The feed is filtered in part c.
  - **Sources (comics and manga):** Open Library search with `fields=...,subject`, keeping only results whose subjects put them in the universe (`searchOpenLibraryIn()`; asks for 3x as many since comics are a small part), plus Google Books (`searchGoogleBooksIn()`: `printType=books`, `q=WORDS subject:"comics"` or `subject:"manga"`, `orderBy=relevance|newest`), taking turns, without repeats (`withoutRepeats()`). Popular/newest/genre lists: `fetchUniverseBooks(universe, genre, limit, offset, "popular"|"newest")` (Open Library `subject:"comics" OR ...` sorted by editions or new, plus Google). Books mode leaves comics and manga out of Open Library lists too (subjects checked where Open Library sends them; its weekly trending list sometimes has none). **AniList is not used** (owner's decision, October 2026): its terms forbid manga list/tracker sites. **Series vs volumes:** each Open Library work and each Google Books volume is one book; volumes aren't split or merged (a Google Books result is often a single volume, like "Berserk Vol. 1").
  - **Google Books books:** id `GB` + Google's volume id (`GBzyTCAlFPjgYC`; database rule `^(OL[0-9]+W|GB[A-Za-z0-9_-]{6,20})$`, `step24`), page `book/GB...`. `fetchBookDetails()` picks the source; `fetchBookFromGoogle()` gives title, authors, cover (https, no page curl), ISBN, page count and genres from Google's categories ("Comics & Graphic Novels / Manga / Fantasy"). Google's terms: credit (footer: "Book data and covers from Open Library and Google Books"), a "View on Google Books" link on the book page (`showGoogleDetails()` in book.js), and no permanent copies, so we save title, author, ISBN, cover link and genres when someone logs it but **never the description**: it loads live (`fetchGoogleDescription()`, Google's HTML read as text, never put in as HTML). The author name isn't linked for Google books (no Open Library author). No API key (Google allows keyless calls from each visitor's browser, with a lower limit per visitor).
  - **Opening a book from another universe** (shared link, search, post) switches to the book's universe in place (`matchUniverse()` in book.js, `showUniverse()`); Back to Books still works.
  - **Adult content:** never shown. Google's `MATURE` books are dropped, and anything whose title, subjects or categories match `ADULT_WORDS` (erotic, hentai, porn, ecchi, smut, sexually explicit, adult comics...) in every Open Library and Google list (`looksAdult()`, `fitsUniverse()`).
  - **Posts, feed, Book of the week, weekly question (part c):** `posts.universe`, `book_picks.universe`, `weekly_prompts.universe` (`books`/`comics`/`manga`, default `books`, `step25-universes-social.sql`).   - **Spotlight cards (part d):** admin news for one universe (`spotlights`: title 1-120, note up to 600, optional `link_url` (https only, shown as "Read more on SITE ↗" with `rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow"`; we never copy the article), universe; `spotlight_books`: up to 6 books in order, position 1-6).   - **Profiles:** everything someone logged shows by default; a small All / Books / Comics / Manga filter above their list (`setUpUniverseFilter()` in profile.js). Reading goal, diary, year in review and stats count everything.
- **Book to screen (films and TV series based on books, comics and manga):** data from **Wikidata** only (free, CC0; TMDB is not used because its free licence is non-commercial and ads are planned). Text only: no posters, no logos.
  - **Data:** `tools/make-adaptations.mjs`, run daily at 03:41 UTC and on demand by `.github/workflows/adaptations.yml` ("Refresh book to screen", Run workflow button, 30 minute limit). It loads the site's own `sources.js`, `universe-seeds.js` and `js/screen-data.js` in Node (same rules and keys as the site) and asks Wikidata's query service, one query at a time with a User-Agent naming reddle.org and hello@reddle.org (Wikidata's rule), each query split into films and series so it stays under Wikidata's 60 second limit, up to 5 tries each (honours Retry-After), 75 s per try. Films/series = instance of film, animated film, feature film, anime film, TV film, TV series, animated series, miniseries, anime series, web series, with "based on" (P144) a work that has an author (P50), a creator (P170) or an Open Library id (P648, only `OL...W`). Labels in English, else the language-neutral one; films without either are skipped. Dates: earliest publication date (P577) or series start (P580) with Wikidata's precision (day, month or year); announcement date (P6949) for announced ones. Wikipedia links only to real `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/` articles, else the Wikidata page. Adult films are left out: Wikidata genre (P136) pornographic film, hentai, erotic, pink, softcore or sexploitation film or a narrower kind of one (305 such adaptations in October 2026, mostly "XXX parodies"), titles with "XXX", and `looksAdult()` titles. The source's own Wikidata kind (instance of, P31) decides the universe: manga/manhwa/manhua/webtoon kinds (`MANGA_TYPES`) = Manga, comic book series/comics/graphic novel/webcomic/comics character (`COMICS_TYPES`) = Comics (Open Library adds the other genres; many manga have no Open Library subjects at all); a source that is only a film, TV series, franchise, game, channel or screen character (`NOT_WRITTEN`) is left out. ## Database tables

Defined in `supabase/schema.sql` (run once in the Supabase SQL Editor).

- **profiles:** id (same as the login user id), username, bio, avatar_url, is_private (`step20`), onboarded (has seen the welcome page; `step13-onboarding.sql`), favorite_genres (from the taste questionnaire; `step15-favorite-genres.sql`)
- **books:** id, external_id (unique), title, author, cover_url, description, isbn, genres (list of genre slugs; `step14-genres.sql`), page_count (`step18`)
- **books_with_stats** (a view, not a table): each book plus log_count, avg_rating, rating_count and logs_this_week. Use it for anything ranked (genre pages, recommendations, popular).
- **logs:** id, user_id, book_id, rating, review, date_read (unique per user + book), spoiler, reread_count, reread_dates (`step18`)
- **follows:** follower_id, following_id
- **old_usernames:** username (old name), user_id. Filled by a trigger whenever someone changes their username, so old profile links still work. In `supabase/step8-old-usernames.sql`.
- **admins:** user_id. Only the owner (`cflo13`). Users can only see their own row (to show the Admin menu link). In `supabase/step10-reports-admin.sql`, which has a placeholder id; the live project has the owner's real id.
- **likes:** user_id, log_id (one per user + log), created_at. Everyone can read; you can only add or remove your own. **log_like_counts** (a view): log_id, like_count. In `supabase/step17-likes.sql`.
- **reading_goals:** user_id, year, target (one per user + year). Everyone can read; you can only change your own. In `supabase/step19-reading-goals.sql`.
- **follow_requests:** requester_id, target_id (only to private profiles; both people can see and remove it). **blocks:** blocker_id, blocked_id. **mutes:** muter_id, muted_id (only the owner sees their own blocks/mutes). All in `supabase/step20-privacy-safety.sql`, with the helper checks `is_blocked_pair()`, `can_see_activity()`, `can_see_profile()`, `log_owner()`, `my_blocks_and_mutes()` and the spam-limit trigger.
- **email_prefs:** user_id, new_followers, follow_requests, comments (on by default), unsubscribe_token. Own row only. **email_log:** event_key, recipient_id, sent_at (only the Edge Function uses it). In `supabase/step21-email-notifications.sql`.
- **feedback:** user_id (empty for logged-out visitors), message (1 to 2000), page, created_at, kind (`bug`/`idea`/`other`), steps, picture_path (must be in the sender's own folder), device_info, screen_width, installed_app, status (`new`/`looking`/`fixed`/`wont_fix`, always `new` on insert), contact_email (required when there's no user). Anyone logged in can send their own; visitors can send with an email and no picture; only admins can read, change the status or delete. Deleting an account deletes its reports. In `supabase/step16-feedback.sql` + `step23-bug-reports.sql` (also the private `feedback-pictures` bucket: upload to your own folder; admins and the sender can see/delete).
- **reports:** reporter_id, log_id or reported_user_id (exactly one), reason (max 500), status (`open` / `dismissed`). Anyone logged in can send; only admins can read, dismiss or delete. One report per person per review/profile. Admins may also delete any log and any avatar (`is_admin()` in the rules).
- **shelves:** user_id, book_id, status (`want_to_read` or `currently_reading`), one shelf per user + book, current_page (`step18`). In `supabase/step7-shelves.sql`.

Everyone can read everything (except reports, admins, feedback, other people's blocks/mutes/follow requests, and the activity of private or blocked profiles: see Private profiles). Users can only change their own profile, logs, follows and shelves.

**Limits live in the database too** (schema.sql + step12-limits.sql): usernames, bio 300, review 5000, ratings in half steps, report reason 500, avatars only from our storage, books only with Open Library ids. If you change a limit in the browser code, change the SQL too.
Any signed-in user can save a new book.
