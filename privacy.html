Privacy policy

Last updated 15 October 2026

This page explains, in plain English, what Reddle keeps about you, who can see it, and how to delete it. It covers the website (reddle.org) and the Reddle app for Android, which shows the same website inside an app.

What we keep

  • Your account: your email address and a password. Passwords are scrambled (hashed) by our login provider, so nobody at Reddle can read them.
  • Your profile: your username, bio and profile picture, if you add them.
  • What you do on Reddle: the books you log, your ratings, reviews (and whether they contain spoilers), dates read and rereads, the page you're on in books you're reading, your yearly reading goal, your Want to read and Currently reading shelves, the reviews and posts you like, and who you follow.
  • Posts and comments: the thoughts you share (with the book and picture you attach, if any), your answers to the weekly question, and your comments on reviews, posts and the Book of the week. Pictures you attach are resized in your browser and stored as public files, so anyone with the link can see them; deleting the post deletes the picture.
  • Your reading taste: the genres you pick in the taste questionnaire, which we use to suggest books, and the genres you choose to hide (Settings, Hidden genres). If you're logged in, the hidden list is saved with your profile, like your favourite genres; if not, it stays only in your browser.
  • Reports: if you report a review, profile, post or comment, we keep the report and who sent it, so we can look into it.
  • Privacy choices: whether your profile is private, the follow requests you send and receive, and the people you block or mute. Only you can see who you block or mute; they aren't told.
  • Feedback and bug reports: if you send one, we keep what you wrote, what kind it is (bug, idea or something else), the screenshot if you add one, who sent it, and a few details sent with it automatically: the page address, your browser and device in a few words (like "Chrome 129 on Android"), your screen width, and whether you're using the installed app. Nothing else is collected. If you aren't logged in, we also keep the email address you type, and only use it to reply to that report. Only the site owner can read reports and see screenshots; deleting a report deletes its screenshot, and deleting your account deletes your reports.
  • Google sign in: if you use it, Google tells us your email address, your name and a link to your Google profile picture. We use the email address to log you in; we don't show your name or Google picture anywhere.
  • Technical logs: when your browser or the app talks to our database, our provider Supabase keeps short technical logs (the time, what was asked for, and your IP address) for a few days, to keep the service running and safe from abuse. GitHub Pages, which hosts the website, keeps similar logs. We don't use them to follow what you do.

What's public

Reddle is a social site, so your profile is public: your username, bio, picture, logs, ratings, reviews, rereads, reading progress, reading goal, diary and year in review, shelves, likes, and who you follow and who follows you. Anyone can see these, even without an account.

Private profiles: in Settings you can make your profile private. Then only people you approve can see your books, shelves, reading goal and likes; your username, picture and bio stay visible so friends can find you. People you block can't see your profile at all.

Your email address is never shown to other people.

Where it's stored

  • Your account, profile, picture and activity are stored with Supabase, the service that runs our database and logins. Supabase also sends the emails that confirm your sign up and reset your password.
  • The website itself is hosted on GitHub Pages. Everything between your device, the website and Supabase travels over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).
  • Book details and covers are loaded from Open Library and Google Books (Google Books mostly for comics and manga). Your browser contacts them directly when it shows or searches for a book, so they can see your IP address and what you searched for, like any website you visit. When someone logs a book, we keep its title, author, cover link, ISBN and genres; for Google Books books we don't keep the description (it's loaded from Google each time).
  • Film and TV information (the "On screen" lists and the Book to screen page) comes from Wikidata. We copy it to our own site once a day, so your browser never contacts Wikidata. Its titles link to Wikipedia, which only sees you if you open one.
  • Reading news shows headlines from other sites (their names are on each headline). Once a day we copy only the headline, the site's name, the date and the link to our own site; we don't keep their articles. Opening a headline takes you to that site, which then sees your visit like any other. Where a site allows us to show its own small picture next to a headline, your browser loads that picture straight from the site (without telling it which Reddle page you were on), so it can see your IP address.

What we don't do

  • We don't show ads.
  • We don't sell or rent your information to anyone, and we don't share it with anyone except the services above that run Reddle for us.
  • We don't use analytics or tracking tools, in the website or the app.
  • We don't use tracking or advertising cookies. Your browser stores a few small things on your device: a login token so you stay logged in, your day or night mode choice, which universe you're in (Books, Comics or Manga), which feed tab you last used, the genres you hid, when you last opened your notifications (for the number on the bell), the page to return to after logging in, whether you closed the "Reddle on your home screen" card, and saved copies of Reddle's own pages so the app works offline. None of it is sent to anyone else.

Your choices

  • You can change your username, email and password, edit your bio and picture, and edit or delete any log, at any time.
  • You can delete your account in Settings. This deletes your profile, picture, logs, reviews, posts and their pictures, comments, likes, shelves, follows, and the reports and feedback you sent, straight away. Books stay on Reddle because they're shared, but nothing on them is linked to you any more. If you can't log in, email us and we'll delete it for you. How to delete your account explains every step.

Children

Reddle is for people aged 13 and over. If you're under 13, please don't sign up.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we'll update the date at the top and mention it on the site.

Questions

Email hello@reddle.org, or see the contact details on the About page.
